# 50.2 — Context budgeting

## Scope

[DERIVED] Context budgeting chooses evidence representations, order and retained history under a model's exact serialized-token limit and a broader compute/latency budget. We develop allocation, deduplication, compression, summaries and source retention. The objective is useful supported output, not the largest possible context or the highest compression ratio. Chapter 49 supplies ranked candidates; [50.1](50-1-context-assembly.md) supplies the typed compiler and source map.

## Why this exists

[DERIVED] Ranked evidence has variable length, redundancy, authority and complementarity. Two short passages may jointly establish a conclusion that neither supports alone; one long passage may contain both the relevant rule and its exception. Selecting by relevance per token can delete the exception, while keeping all near-duplicates can displace a necessary second fact. The token budget constrains a structured reasoning problem rather than a bag of independently useful fragments.

[DERIVED] Compression adds a second uncertainty. A shorter prompt can preserve broad topic and lose exact numbers, negation, attribution or temporal scope. A summary generated by the same model may convert an uncertainty into a confident statement. Its source reference must remain attached, but provenance does not prove that the summary preserves the source's meaning. Budgeting therefore needs both a representation policy and an independently assessable preservation criterion.

## Intuition

[DERIVED] First reserve mandatory instructions, the current request, required tool/history dependencies and output capacity. Then choose evidence bundles with sufficient provenance. Deduplicate exact copies without merging incompatible versions. Use compression where its semantic risk and processing cost are acceptable, and verify the final rendered token count. The compiler can return infeasible when mandatory content already consumes the window; that is an informative result.

## Formulation

[DERIVED] Let $K$ be mandatory items and $S$ optional evidence, with representation choices $z$, ordering $\sigma$, compiler $\tau$, and answer loss $\ell$. A conceptual design objective is

$$
 \min_{S,z,\sigma}\space
 \mathbb E[\ell(Y,q)\mid \operatorname{compile}_\tau(K,S,z,\sigma)]
 +\lambda C(S,z,\sigma),
 \quad
 L_\tau(K,S,z,\sigma)+Y_{\max}\le W .
$$

(Eq. 50.4)

[DERIVED] $C$ includes retrieval, compression and model execution cost under a declared unit. The expectation is generally unknown, so practical policies optimize proxies. Relevance, coverage and redundancy are candidate proxies, not interchangeable estimates of answer loss. Treating item costs as additive token counts is an analytical approximation; final template serialization must still satisfy Eq. 50.2.

[MATHEMATICALLY-DERIVED] A useful fixed-round coverage proxy has nonnegative facet weights $a_z$, coverage coefficients $w_{dz}\in[0,1]$, and nonnegative modular relevance $b_d$:
$$
 F(S)=\sum_z a_z\left[1-\prod_{d\in S}(1-w_{dz})\right]+\sum_{d\in S}b_d,
 \quad
 \Delta(d\mid S)=\sum_z a_zw_{dz}\prod_{e\in S}(1-w_{ez})+b_d .
$$

(Eq. 50.5)

[MATHEMATICALLY-DERIVED] For $A\subseteq B$ and $d\notin B$, every extra factor in $B$ lies in $[0,1]$, so $\Delta(d\mid A)\ge\Delta(d\mid B)\ge0$. Thus $F$ is normalized, monotone nondecreasing and submodular. It need not be strictly increasing: zero weights or already-covered facets can yield zero gain. The 2026 context-allocation paper's fixed-feedback objective belongs to this family, with historical utilization discounting its coefficients. [R50.10, Appendix A.1]

[MATHEMATICALLY-DERIVED] For integer $k\ge1$, under a cardinality constraint $|S|\le k$, with $k$ at most the candidate count (or zero-gain dummy padding), let $O$ be an optimum and $S_j$ the greedy set after $j$ additions. Submodularity gives
$F(O)-F(S_j)\le k\max_d\Delta(d\mid S_j)$.
Consequently,
$$
 F(O)-F(S_{j+1})\le(1-1/k)[F(O)-F(S_j)],
 \qquad F(S_k)\ge[1-(1-1/k)^k]F(O)\ge(1-e^{-1})F(O).
$$

(Eq. 50.6)

[DERIVED] The guarantee applies to this proxy with fixed coefficients and equal item cardinality. It does not prove optimal adaptive allocation, variable-token selection, or actual answer utility. Mandatory evidence can be handled through the residual $F(K\cup S)-F(K)$ with a residual cardinality budget. Recomputing utilization after each generated answer changes the objective between rounds; the proof remains intra-round.

[MATHEMATICALLY-DERIVED] Complementary evidence invalidates diminishing returns. Let useful output require both facts $a$ and $b$, so $U(\varnothing)=U(\{a\})=U(\{b\})=0$ and $U(\{a,b\})=1$. Then $\Delta_U(b\mid\varnothing)=0<1=\Delta_U(b\mid\{a\})$. Conflicting or distracting evidence can also make utility nonmonotone. A coverage proxy should not be advertised as a theorem about such tasks.

## Mechanism

[DERIVED] Deduplication has several meanings. Exact byte/hash duplicates can share storage while retaining access and revision identities. Overlapping chunks can be merged only if their intervals and document version agree. Semantically similar passages can still differ in a number, exception, speaker or valid time. Similarity-based removal is therefore a hypothesis about redundant task information, not a safe identity operation.

[DERIVED] Evidence ordering changes positional access and the relationship between claims and local citation markers. Preserve authoritative exceptions and dependencies near the facts they qualify; record the order in the manifest. Position effects are measured properties of particular models/tasks, not a universal rule that placing evidence at an edge guarantees use. Reordering also changes prefix-cache reuse and prefill work, so equal selected items need not imply equal serving cost.

[DERIVED] Extractive compression retains selected source spans and a direct interval map. Abstractive summaries need parent-span lineage and a separate support check. Both must preserve units, negation, qualification, temporal scope and conflicting assertions when relevant. A model's high surprisal is a proxy for unpredictability under that model, not a sufficient statistic for task importance: a predictable “not” can determine the answer.

[DERIVED] A principled preservation target can compare output distributions for fixed query and full/compressed context. If $P$ and $Q$ are those distributions, $D_{\rm KL}(P\Vert Q)\le\varepsilon$ measured in nats, and $0\le\ell\le1$, Pinsker's inequality yields
$$
 |\mathbb E_P[\ell]-\mathbb E_Q[\ell]|
 \le\operatorname{TV}(P,Q)\le\sqrt{\varepsilon/2}.
$$

(Eq. 50.7)

[DERIVED] This conditional implication is not an achieved compressor guarantee. Estimating sequence-level KL for a black-box target is difficult, and preserving the original model's distribution can preserve its errors. LLMLingua's surrogate token scores approximate a different operational objective. Its printed token-selection inequality and high-perplexity prose use inconsistent probability notation; this chapter's preservation analysis does not silently repair that expression or claim code confirmation.

[PAPER-REPORTED] The 2026 allocation method discounts already-used documents and semantic facets using leave-one-out generation sensitivity. Its fixed-round submodular proxy selects a new context; a separate contrastive decoder can steer later outputs. The feedback is computed with teacher-forced passes on a fixed generated answer. Deleting evidence measures likelihood sensitivity, not automatically factual entailment or an exclusive record of evidence use. [R50.10, §§4.1, 6 and Appendix A.1]

## Algorithm

### Algorithm 50.2 — Dependency-preserving context allocation

[DERIVED] Algorithm 50.2 is a bounded budgeting heuristic, with no approximation claim for true answer utility. Inputs are finite candidates $R$, mandatory set $K$, positive estimated item costs, exact compiler $\tau$, transform budget $B$ and at most $J$ rebuilds. Before any scoring/compression model sees payload, validate authorization/schema for $K$ and candidates; an invalid mandatory item returns failure and rejected optional items are excluded. Initialize $S=K$, remaining budget $B_{\rm rem}=B$, and candidate pool $U=R\setminus K$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
1.\;&L_\tau(K)+Y_{\max}>W\Rightarrow\text{return mandatory-context infeasibility}.\\
2.\;&\text{Visit optional candidates once: invalid identity/access}\Rightarrow\text{exclude from }U\text{ and continue;}\\
 &\text{remove exact duplicate payloads while preserving authorized source aliases}.\\
3.\;&\text{While }U\ne\varnothing\text{ and estimated capacity remains, select a declared feasible bundle;}\\
 &\text{no feasible bundle}\Rightarrow\text{stop};\
 \text{remove the selected bundle from }U\text{ so it is visited once}.\\
4.\;&\text{If transformation is needed, reserve its enforced cost cap from }B_{\rm rem};\\
 &\text{insufficient budget}\Rightarrow\text{skip bundle without transformation};\\
 &\text{otherwise transform with timeout, reconcile actual cost and check preservation};\\
 &\text{failed check}\Rightarrow\text{discard transformed output; skip bundle};\\
 &\text{otherwise retain transformed bundle with parent-span provenance}.\\
5.\;&\text{Add approved bundle to }S;\ \text{render and count exactly};\\
 &\text{overflow}\Rightarrow\text{remove only optional items by the declared rule, at most }J\text{ times};\\
 &\text{still infeasible}\Rightarrow\text{return explicit failure}.\\
6.\;&\text{Return compiled context, ordering, aliases, exclusions and consumed budgets}.
\end{aligned}
$$

(Eq. 50.8)

[DERIVED] Finite candidates, transformation timeouts and a rebuild cap bound the procedure. The invariant is preservation of mandatory dependencies and source lineage; a heuristic gain never overrides access or semantic compatibility. A zero/negative cost estimate is rejected before a gain-per-cost calculation. Empty optional evidence can produce an explicit insufficient-evidence context rather than an invented answer.

## Implementation

[DERIVED] AI_REFERENCE_STACK §4 **#26 Hugging Face Transformers**, layer **Model definition / adaptation**, and **#17 PyTorch**, layer **Model / autograd framework**, supply tokenization and surrogate/target model operators; **#41 vLLM**, layer **Inference engine**, executes compatible generated prompts. Microsoft Research / Microsoft AI §1 #13 routes LLMLingua. The provenance compiler and fixed-round evidence allocator are separate synthesized components.

[DERIVED] Greedy coverage evaluation over $N$ candidates, budget $k$ and $Z$ facets costs $O(NkZ)$ with straightforward gain computation; a cached residual product can reduce repeated work. Dense facet coefficients occupy $O(NZ)$ values. Tokenizing/rendering inspected text is proportional to payload size, while semantic deduplication can require quadratic pair comparisons unless an indexed candidate stage is used. These costs exist before any target-model savings.

[DERIVED] Compression adds surrogate prefill/token-scoring FLOPs, weights, KV capacity and host/device traffic; iterative prefix scoring changes the cost relative to one pass. Summarization adds generated tokens and sequential latency. The target saves work only if the shorter final representation reduces its actual execution path. Break-even requires target work avoided to exceed compression, validation and transfer cost, measured under equal answer-quality criteria. Compression ratio alone is neither latency speedup nor monetary savings.

## Experimental design

### Methodology

[PAPER-REPORTED] LLMLingua evaluates reasoning, conversation and summarization with named target-model revisions and surrogate models. Its component ablations change iterative scoring, budget control and alignment. The tasks and preservation metrics differ; answer exact match and text-overlap similarity do not establish identical semantic preservation. [R50.3, §§5.1 and 5.4]

### Reported experiments

[PAPER-REPORTED] Table 2 reports BBH exact match falling from 70.07 with the full prompt to 56.85 at the reported sevenfold compression condition. Table 3's GSM8K ablation gives 79.08 for the full method versus 72.93 without iterative compression. These are source-specific quality tradeoffs, not universal compression guarantees. [R50.3, Tables 2–3]

[PAPER-REPORTED] The 2026 allocation study freezes evidence pools, tunes on 40 ASQA development queries and evaluates 100 held-out queries per task across four tasks, three generators and two decoding seeds. Table 11's scheduler-only portfolio recall is .300 versus deep rotation .303, with BH-adjusted $q=.717$; the scheduler improves several selection baselines but does not demonstrate recall superiority over deep rotation. It offers fewer distinct documents under its measured policy. [R50.10, §§7.1–7.4 and Table 11]

[DERIVED] Equal $kT$ document slots do not equalize token lengths, teacher-forced probing, decoding work or latency. Portfolio recall also credits facts appearing anywhere across multiple responses; it is not correctness or completeness of one final answer. Those distinctions limit what the source's allocation experiments settle.

## Observations

**What the paper claims.** [PAPER-REPORTED] The allocation paper proposes feedback-driven scheduling with an intra-round submodular guarantee and reports improved portfolio coverage against selection baselines. [R50.10, §§6–8 and Appendix A.1]

**What the evidence shows.** [PAPER-REPORTED] Its fixed-round proof is valid under nonnegative bounded coverage coefficients. Held-out results include nonsignificant recall contrasts against deep rotation, and its budgets are document/generation configurations rather than complete compute equivalence. [R50.10, Tables 10–11]

**What we infer.** [DERIVED] Coverage objectives offer transparent heuristics. Their approximation guarantees must remain attached to the surrogate objective, while practical decisions need task preservation and complete resource accounting.

**What remains unknown.** [NOT-DISCLOSED] No inspected result establishes globally optimal context allocation for complementary/conflicting facts, arbitrary token costs or adaptive long-horizon objectives. This manuscript has no executed compressor or allocator benchmark.

## Failure modes

[DERIVED] Deduplication can erase contradictory versions. Compression can delete negation or units. Summaries can invent certainty or lose attribution. A fixed-round greedy guarantee can be incorrectly transferred to variable-length knapsack selection. A compiler can meet its token budget while exceeding a wall-clock deadline through too many compression/probe calls. Self-evaluation can accept transformations that preserve the model's preferred story rather than the evidence.

## Siblings

[DERIVED] Truncation is cheap but position-dependent. Extractive selection preserves exact spans but can break dependencies. Abstractive summaries improve density with an added support risk. Token pruning retains fragments with model-dependent usefulness. Iterative context rotation exposes more candidates but adds generations and may reach low-relevance ranks. The appropriate policy depends on evidence structure and quality/resource constraints.

## Extensions

### Improvements

[DERIVED] The lineage is component-sensitive budgeting, dependency-aware compression, and feedback-driven fixed-round selection. An unresolved question is whether allocation objectives can represent complementary facts and conflicts while retaining tractable, useful guarantees. Settling evidence would compare explicit bundle/dependency models against coverage proxies on independently labeled tasks with equal complete compute and final-answer criteria.

## Limitations

[DERIVED] Eq. 50.7 assumes an unmeasured distributional bound; Eq. 50.6 assumes a particular proxy and cardinality constraint. Neither proves preservation of real-world truth. The practical algorithm deliberately exposes infeasibility and unknown preservation rather than converting proxy scores into guarantees.

Reproduction needs full/compressed serialized prompts, tokenizers/templates, protected spans, parent references, surrogate/target revisions, scoring convention, facet weights, frozen feedback, candidate costs, rebuild limits and measured quality/cost boundaries.

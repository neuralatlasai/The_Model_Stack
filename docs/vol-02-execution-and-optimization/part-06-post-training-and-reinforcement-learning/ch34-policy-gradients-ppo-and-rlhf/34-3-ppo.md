# 34.3 — PPO

## Scope

[DERIVED] This section owns the PPO surrogate, clipped policy/value losses, temporal-difference residuals, generalized advantage estimation, batch reuse and stopping diagnostics. It separates the exact estimator at the behavior policy from the finite-update approximation. The output is a bounded rollout-to-update procedure whose masks, targets and reduction are inspectable. It does not promise a hard KL constraint or monotonically improving model behavior.

## Why this exists

[DERIVED] PPO is frequently described as keeping the new policy near the old one. Its clipped objective instead removes selected incentives beyond sign-dependent ratio thresholds. Shared parameters can still move clipped actions, and unobserved vocabulary mass can move substantially. Several update epochs reuse prefixes collected under one policy while evaluating another. That is a useful optimization design, but its approximation must not be disguised as a constrained-policy theorem.

[DERIVED] Advantage estimation is equally consequential. A critic at a true terminal state should contribute zero continuation. A valid administrative cutoff can retain a continuation value while stopping the sampled eligibility trace. Collapsing these two flags changes targets throughout the preceding response. Whitening advantages, clipping values and changing loss aggregation are additional interventions; a single label such as “standard PPO” does not specify them.

## Intuition

[MATHEMATICALLY-DERIVED] A positive advantage encourages increasing the sampled action probability. Once its ratio exceeds the upper threshold, the clipped objective stops rewarding further increase from that sample. A negative advantage encourages decreasing probability; below the lower threshold it stops rewarding further decrease. Movement in the corrective direction remains active. Clipping therefore depends jointly on ratio and advantage sign, rather than merely on whether the ratio lies outside an interval.

[MATHEMATICALLY-DERIVED] GAE combines one-step critic residuals over a sampled continuation. Small trace decay relies more on local value estimates; decay one telescopes into a Monte Carlo return minus the starting value on a complete episode. An inaccurate state-only starting baseline need not bias the expected score estimator in that complete Monte Carlo case. This differs from claiming that every individual estimated advantage is accurate.

## Formulation

[MATHEMATICALLY-DERIVED] For detached behavior log probabilities and advantage estimates, define the selected-action ratio and the basic clipped maximization objective:

$$
\rho_t=\exp(\ell_{\theta,t}-\ell_{b,t}),\qquad L_{\rm clip}(\theta)=\frac1Z\sum_{i,t}m_{it}\min\!\left(\rho_{it}\widehat A_{it},\operatorname{clip}(\rho_{it},1-\epsilon_-,1+\epsilon_+)\widehat A_{it}\right).
$$
*(Eq. 34.9)*

where $m$ selects assistant decisions, $Z$ is the explicitly chosen fixed batch normalization, and $\epsilon_-,\epsilon_+>0$ can be asymmetric. Both scores must describe the same token event and declared policy processors.

[MATHEMATICALLY-DERIVED] Compress observation tokens out of the decision clock. Let $b_t$ admit a continuation bootstrap and $c_t$ admit the next *sampled* decision from the same episode. At a censored last action, $b_t=1$ can coexist with $c_t=0$:

$$
\delta_t=r_t+\gamma b_t\,v_{\phi_b}(s_{t+1})-v_{\phi_b}(s_t),\qquad \widehat A_t=\delta_t+\gamma\lambda_{\rm GAE}c_t\widehat A_{t+1},\qquad \widehat A_{K+1}=0.
$$
*(Eq. 34.10)*

where $K$ is the final collected assistant decision, $v_{\phi_b}$ is the frozen pre-update critic, $0\leq\lambda_{\rm GAE}\leq1$ is local trace decay, and all targets are detached. An unavailable continuation state cannot be invented for bootstrapping.

## Mechanism

[MATHEMATICALLY-DERIVED] With a complete episode, uninterrupted trace, trace decay one and terminal bootstrap zero, the value terms cancel. With a valid censored continuation at $K$, the last value remains:

$$
\widehat A_t\big|_{\lambda_{\rm GAE}=1}=\sum_{k=t}^{K}\gamma^{k-t}r_k+\gamma^{K-t+1}b_Kv_{\phi_b}(s_{K+1})-v_{\phi_b}(s_t),\qquad \widehat Y_t=\widehat A_t+v_{\phi_b}(s_t).
$$
*(Eq. 34.11)*

where interior transitions have $b_k=c_k=1$ and no episode boundary is crossed. For a true terminal $b_K=0$, the target becomes the observed return; for a continuing cutoff it retains a bootstrap approximation.

[MATHEMATICALLY-DERIVED] A scalar critic can minimize a masked squared target error. A conservative clipped-value variant takes the larger error from current and clipped predictions:

$$
L_v=\frac1{2Z_v}\sum_{i,t}m_{it}\max\!\left((v_\phi-\widehat Y)^2,\left(v_{\phi_b}+\operatorname{clip}(v_\phi-v_{\phi_b},-\epsilon_v,\epsilon_v)-\widehat Y\right)^2\right).
$$
*(Eq. 34.12)*

where all values and targets refer to the same pre-action state. This clips a branch of the loss, not the actual value prediction or the optimizer step. $Z_v$ is declared separately from the actor reduction.

[MATHEMATICALLY-DERIVED] A three-action counterexample rules out a hard KL guarantee from selected-action clipping. Let behavior be $(0.2,0.4,0.4)$ and a new policy be $(0.2,0.8-\eta,\eta)$ with $0<\eta<0.8$. If the batch observes only the first action, its ratio remains exactly one for every $\eta$, while the full-state divergence $\mathrm{KL}(\mu\|\pi)$ grows without bound as $\eta\to0$. Even when more actions are observed, clipping does not project parameter updates into a feasible KL set. A separately evaluated divergence and an explicit stopping rule can detect drift; they do not retroactively turn the clipped objective into constrained optimization.

[DERIVED] Multi-epoch reuse increases departure from the behavior prefix distribution. Early updates can be approximately local; subsequent updates compound ratio changes and critic-target mismatch. Frozen advantages prevent a moving target inside the actor loop, but they become less representative of the new policy. The number of epochs, minibatch order and stop criterion therefore belong in the estimator approximation contract. Smaller learning rates are a practical control, not a proof of locality.

## Algorithm

### Algorithm 34.3 — bounded PPO batch update

[DERIVED] Inputs to Algorithm 34.3 are sealed episodes, actor/reference/critic versions, separate bootstrap and trace flags, clip widths, finite epoch cap and drift thresholds. State contains frozen targets, current parameters, optimizer states and an update counter. Outputs are an accepted checkpoint or a named aborted update with unchanged collection provenance.

$$
\begin{aligned}
&1.\quad \text{verify action scores, support, boundary flags and global reduction mass.}\\
&2.\quad \widehat A_{K+1}\leftarrow0;\ \textbf{for }t=K,\ldots,1:\ \text{compute Eq.34.10 within each episode}.\\
&3.\quad \widehat Y_t\leftarrow\operatorname{stopgrad}(\widehat A_t+v_{\phi_b}(s_t));\ \widehat A\leftarrow\operatorname{stopgrad}(\widehat A).\\
&4.\quad \textbf{for }e=1,\ldots,E_{\max}:\ \textbf{for each declared minibatch:}\\
&5.\qquad \ell_\theta\leftarrow\operatorname{Score}_{\theta}(s,a);\ \rho\leftarrow\exp(\ell_\theta-\ell_b).\\
&6.\qquad \text{optimize }-L_{\rm clip}+c_vL_v\text{ with declared separate/shared parameters.}\\
&7.\qquad \text{if nonfinite state or declared drift/gradient limit fails, abort or stop as specified.}\\
&8.\quad \text{return checkpoint, exact epochs/steps, reductions, drift traces and rejection ledger.}
\end{aligned}
$$

[DERIVED] Invariants prohibit crossing episode boundaries, updating behavior scores and differentiating targets through the actor loss. Finite minibatches and $E_{\max}$ guarantee stopping. Aborting after a nonfinite optimizer step requires a pre-step checkpoint to restore state; stopping after an accepted finite step is a different operation. Compute includes actor forward/backward for every reuse pass and critic computation according to its update schedule. The algorithm does not invent a monotonic-improvement certificate.

## Implementation

[DERIVED] Implementation route: **verl** (reference-stack §4 #37, **RL post-training**; §4.1 **Post-training / RL**), pinned at v0.9.1. The role-specific mechanism and its evidence boundary are stated below.

[OFFICIAL-DOCUMENTATION] At the pinned verl version, the function named `compute_policy_loss_vanilla` also includes a dual-clip branch for negative advantages and clamps log ratios before exponentiation. Its default name therefore does not identify only Eq.34.9. The GAE helper whitens advantages after creating returns. Its end-value initialization is zero, so a continuing cutoff requires an explicit upstream contract or a different target path. [R34.1, named functions](references.md#r34-1)

[DERIVED] Align the critic state with the logits that predicted each assistant token; an off-by-one shift can fit values to states that already include the chosen action. Keep pre-whitening advantages and returns for diagnosis. Whitening depends on batch composition and can make an otherwise identical example's update depend on its companions. Record population/sample variance convention, epsilon and whether statistics are global across ranks. Value clipping, actor clipping and gradient-norm clipping are distinct controls with different units.

## Experimental design

[PAPER-REPORTED] SAE changes the GAE trace at uncertainty-defined segment boundaries. Its theorem bounds a critic-error expression under assumptions; a decreasing upper bound is not a guarantee that actual policy-gradient bias decreases. [R34.2, §4, Appendix A–B](references.md#r34-2)

| Protocol | Disclosed setting |
|---|---|
| Model/data | Qwen3-8B-base; DAPO-Math17k |
| Collection | 512 prompts × 8; temperature .6; 8,192 response cap |
| Updates | Actor $10^{-6}$, critic $10^{-5}$; batch 4,096; 600 PPO steps; no KL/entropy loss |
| Evaluation / uncertainty | Six math benchmarks; hardware, precision, seeds NOT-DISCLOSED |
| Budget caveat | GRPO comparison stopped at 400 steps after instability |

[ASSUMED] Proposed finite audit uses a two-step reward ledger, three ending conditions and trace decay zero/one/intermediate. Enumerate targets by hand and compare to the recursion before testing a neural critic. Independently vary ratio, advantage sign and clip asymmetry to verify local derivatives. Keep sampled actions and denominator fixed. Metrics are target error, derivative sign and observed full-state KL; expected clipping behavior is not an expected KL bound.

## Observations

**What the paper claims.** [PAPER-REPORTED] SAE argues that segment-aware traces improve long-response advantage estimates. [R34.2, §4](references.md#r34-2)

**What the evidence shows.** [PAPER-REPORTED] The reported average is 40.98 versus adaptive-trace 38.89, while AMC is 77.56 versus 78.39. That negative comparison prevents a claim of uniform benchmark improvement. Unequal stopped-baseline budgets also limit causal comparison. [R34.2, Table 1, §5](references.md#r34-2)

**What we infer.** [DERIVED] Trace design is a measurable algorithmic choice rather than a constant hidden in configuration. It should be evaluated with boundary correctness and critic calibration, separately from ratio clipping. The source's bounded critic-error model does not replace those implementation checks.

**What remains unknown.** [NOT-DISCLOSED] Hardware and independent-run uncertainty are absent from the inspected protocol. No source experiment establishes a universal best trace decay or guarantees safety under arbitrary censoring.

## Failure modes

[DERIVED] Zeroing a continuing cutoff biases the last residual; propagating it spreads the error backward. Crossing a packed episode boundary contaminates every prior advantage in the trace. Using a post-action state leaks the chosen action into the baseline. Treating clip fraction as full-policy distance hides unseen mass movement. Recomputing old scores changes the denominator policy. Reusing a batch until reward improves on its own samples creates optimizer and selection overfitting.

## Siblings

[DERIVED] Monte Carlo score estimation avoids critic bootstrapping but has higher outcome variance in some workloads. Group-relative methods replace the learned critic with coupled response statistics, covered in Chapter 35. Direct preference optimization uses an offline preference likelihood, covered in Chapter 33. A hard distributional trust-region solver would require an explicit constraint and feasibility procedure absent from basic PPO.

## Extensions

[PAPER-REPORTED] Categorical critics replace scalar regression with a distributional target and retain a scalar expectation for actor advantages. The inspected math arm improves reported averages, but this is not proof that MSE is inconsistent: squared-error regression remains statistically consistent for a conditional mean. Target smoothing, head capacity and optimization change together. [R34.4, §§3–4](references.md#r34-4)

[DERIVED] A categorical head's bins, support interval, target projection and stop-gradient conventions must be declared. It adds logits and parameters even when runtime overhead is small. Segmented traces and categorical critics can be combined only with a joint ablation; gains from separate papers cannot be added arithmetically.

## Limitations

[DERIVED] Approximate advantages, numerical mismatches and finite batch reuse limit the relation to the exact score gradient. Clipping and whitening deliberately alter the update. A counterexample to a hard-KL guarantee does not show PPO is useless; it identifies which guarantee cannot be claimed. Source improvements remain specific to their models, rewards, stopping rules and selected checkpoints.

## Reproducibility

[DERIVED] Publish pre/post-whitening advantages, TD residuals, value targets, boundary flags, clip widths, dual-clip settings, reduction denominators, actual reuse count and full-state or qualified sampled drift metrics. Verify hand-computed recursion and gradient branches. Record whether stopping restores state or accepts the last finite update. Pin code rather than borrowing its configuration name as a complete specification.

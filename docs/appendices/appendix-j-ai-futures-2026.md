A forecast or scenario is a conditional claim about a defined quantity. Before citing one, record (DERIVED · §06.4 measurement uncertainty, §21.6 capability prediction, §61.3 version and protocol control):

1. **Source, version, and date** — scenarios and timeline models are revised; cite the version, not the project.
2. **Target quantity and its operational definition** — "AGI", "superhuman coder", or a task time horizon at a stated success rate are defined differently by different sources.
3. **Resolution criterion** — the observation that would settle the claim, and by when.
4. **Distribution, not a point** — the interval or full distribution, not the median alone.
5. **Inputs and their provenance** — compute growth, algorithmic progress, benchmark trends, and time-horizon doubling times are themselves measurements with protocols (Appendix I; §61.3).
6. **Conditioning assumptions** — what the forecast holds fixed (no pause, no disruption of compute supply, no regulatory intervention).
7. **Forecaster population** — the authors, domain experts, superforecasters, or an aggregate, and any stake in the outcome.
